<!-- SPEAKER 1: KIM ROCKELL -->
<div style="margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 30px;">
  <div style="display: flex; gap: 20px; flex-wrap: wrap; align-items: flex-start;">
    <img src="assets/images/C17C77DD-987A-485D-AD1A-E6A15A3448C5_1_201_a-271x300.jpeg" alt="Kim Rockell" style="width: 140px; height: auto; border-radius: 8px; border: 1px solid #e2e8f0;">
    <div style="flex: 1; min-width: 260px;">
      <h4 style="margin: 0 0 4px 0; color: #0f172a; font-size: 1.2em;">Kim Rockell</h4>
      <p style="margin: 0 0 12px 0; color: #0066cc; font-weight: 600;">Komazawa University</p>
      <p style="font-size: 0.95em; line-height: 1.6; color: #475569; margin: 0;">
        Associate Professor Kim Rockell is an ethnomusicologist and classical guitarist teaching in the Faculty of Arts and Sciences at Komazawa University in Tokyo. His research interests encompass plucked-string traditions, Philippine rondalla and Hispanic musical influences in the Asia Pacific, migrant music and music in diaspora, and the music/language nexus. Recently, while learning traditional Noh in Japan, he has begun exploring the educational potential of English language Noh.
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div style="background-color: #f8fafc; border-left: 3px solid #0066cc; border-radius: 4px; padding: 14px; margin-top: 15px;">
    <strong style="color: #0f172a;">Music and Performance in Education: A Personal Retrospective (2012–2022)</strong>
    <p style="margin: 8px 0 0 0; font-size: 0.9em; line-height: 1.6; color: #334155;">
      This talk traces the trajectory of a decade-long Japan-based research journey on music and performance in education. It introduces past projects exploring the strategies teachers with musical backgrounds bring to language teaching, applications to computer-assisted language learning (CALL), English-medium instruction in Tohoku and Tokyo, and the development of English musical theater based on traditional Noh.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<!-- SPEAKER 2: JAMES CARPENTER -->
<div style="margin-bottom: 30px;">
  <div style="display: flex; gap: 20px; flex-wrap: wrap; align-items: flex-start;">
    <div style="flex: 1; min-width: 260px;">
      <h4 style="margin: 0 0 4px 0; color: #0f172a; font-size: 1.2em;">James Carpenter</h4>
      <p style="margin: 0 0 12px 0; color: #0066cc; font-weight: 600;">Rikkyo University</p>
      <p style="font-size: 0.95em; line-height: 1.6; color: #475569; margin: 0;">
        James Carpenter explores how individuals learn in unique environments and is currently conducting research alongside learners and teachers at a school for the visually impaired in Tokyo.
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div style="background-color: #f8fafc; border-left: 3px solid #0066cc; border-radius: 4px; padding: 14px; margin-top: 15px;">
    <strong style="color: #0f172a;">Researching Communicative Practices in Performance-Based Education</strong>
    <p style="margin: 8px 0 0 0; font-size: 0.9em; line-height: 1.6; color: #334155;">
      This presentation introduces a mixed-methods research approach combining ethnomethodology with quantitative analysis. Drawing on research at a school for the visually impaired, the talk demonstrates how conversation analytic and ethnomethodological perspectives provide useful frameworks for investigating foreign language acquisition in performance-based contexts.
    </p>
  </div>
